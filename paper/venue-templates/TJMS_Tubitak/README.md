## 1. Why (and why not) TJMS

**For it:**
- **Zero fee, confirmed:** "There are no Article Publishing Charges (APC) required for the publication of this journal." Home page: "diamond open-access journal". About page: "The author does not have any publication charges for open access."
- **PubMed and MEDLINE indexed** — the only venue in this playbook with confirmed MEDLINE coverage besides *Burns* and CMPB, and the only *free* one.
- **Web of Science (SCIE)** and **Scopus** (from 1994). Impact Factor (2025) **1.4**, 5-year 1.8, **Q2**.
- You already know the publisher's systems, style guide culture and Editorial Manager workflow.
- A natural lateral move if TJEECS rejects on "not enough electrical engineering / computer science."

**Against it:**
- **Scope fit is weaker than TJEECS's, not stronger.** See §4.
- **The rebuild is large.** LaTeX → Word, 15 pages → 30 double-spaced pages, unstructured → four-heading structured abstract, abbreviated → **full** journal names in references, PDF-only figures → jpeg/tiff.
- Lower Impact Factor than TJEECS (1.4 vs 2.1), though a better quartile (Q2 vs Q3).

- Informed consent, if any

**ORCID:** "All authors are required to provide their ORCID iD during the submission process."

### ⚠️ The ethics statement — TJMS is a medical journal and this is the sharpest edge

TJMS requires you to "provide a statement identifying the ethics committee that approved the study" (Helsinki / US Federal Policy / EMA GCP).

**There is no written exemption for public, de-identified, secondary datasets.** Verified 2026-07-25: no exemption, waiver, retrospective-data or de-identified-data clause appears anywhere in the policy. **UNVERIFIED** whether your situation is accommodated.

What *is* in your favour: both the styleguide and the template phrase the requirement conditionally — "Informed consent, **if any**" — and the ethics requirement is scoped to "experimental investigations conducted with humans."

**Practical approach:**
1. State plainly that no human-subject involvement occurred, that the study is a secondary analysis of publicly available de-identified photographs, and that ethics committee approval was therefore not required.
2. Cite the datasets' own licence and provenance (Roboflow CC BY 4.0; BIP_US provided for research by the University of Seville).
3. **Get a letter from your faculty's ethics committee stating that approval was not required.** For a medical journal this is worth the weeks it takes, and it costs nothing. Request it now, not when an editor asks.
4. If in any doubt, **email medsci@tubitak.gov.tr before submitting** and ask how they want a public-dataset AI study handled. Editors answer this question routinely and it is much cheaper to ask than to be returned.

### The GenAI declaration

TÜBİTAK's publisher-wide policy is unusually demanding, and it is the same policy your paper already satisfies at TJEECS:

- "Full disclosure of AI or AI-assisted tools is mandatory."
- The template asks for **"the name and version of the large language model utilized, along with the exact prompt(s) employed"**, the purpose, and the specific sections where applied.
- "AI tools, including generative models like ChatGPT, cannot be credited as authors."
- "Authors are not permitted to use generative AI for creating or modifying" images or figures (brightness/contrast/colour-balance adjustments are allowed).
- **Non-generative machine learning tools "should be disclosed in the relevant caption."** Relevant to you: your segmentation model is research content, not a writing aid — but if any *figure* was produced by an ML process, note it in that figure's caption.
- "Concealing the use of AI tools is unethical."

**Your existing declaration already meets this, including the verbatim prompt.** Carry it across unchanged. Do not shorten it.

## 4. Scope — the honest assessment

> "The Turkish Journal of Medical Sciences is published electronically 6 times a year by TUBITAK ULAKBIM and accepts original English-language manuscripts in in all fields of medicine and related health sciences." [sic — the doubled "in" is in the original]
> — https://journals.tubitak.gov.tr/medical/aimsandscope.html (verified 2026-07-25)

Followed by a caveat that papers "focusing on the technical details of a given medical subspeciality may not be evaluated."

**Medical informatics and AI-in-medicine are named nowhere** — not in the scope, the keywords, or the article-type list. Your paper would enter under the generic "all fields of medicine and related health sciences."

**Compare TJEECS**, which explicitly lists **machine learning, computer vision, signal/image/video processing, biomedical technology and instrumentation** among its topics. **Your paper sits more comfortably in TJEECS's scope than in TJMS's**, which is worth knowing: TJMS is a lateral move for *audience*, not an upgrade for *fit*. And the "technical details" caveat cuts directly against an architecture-benchmarking paper.

**Implication for the rewrite:** you must convert this into a clinical paper, not merely reformat it. Frame it as *"an evaluation of whether an AI burn-severity assessment tool is safe to use,"* and the answer is no. That is a medical finding. "We compared eleven backbones" is not.
