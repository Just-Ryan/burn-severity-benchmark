## 1. The fee situation — free, if you choose correctly

*Burns* is **hybrid**: "Burns offers authors two choices to publish their research" — open access or subscription.

| | Verbatim | Verified |

| Gold OA APC | "All articles … **USD 3570**" (excluding taxes) | 2026-07-25 |
| **Subscription route** | **"No open access publication fee."** / "No publication fee charged to authors" | 2026-07-25 |
| Review is unaffected | "Your publication choice will have no effect on the peer review process" | 2026-07-25 |

**Choose the subscription route. You pay USD 0.** Your article is paywalled; you get free green OA by self-archiving the accepted manuscript after a **12-month embargo**.

⚠️ **Stale-figure warning:** many secondary sites and the project's own audit still quote **USD 3,190**. The live figure on 2026-07-25 is **USD 3,570**. This is exactly why you re-verify.

⚠️ **The trap:** at acceptance you will be shown a rights form offering CC BY or CC BY-NC-ND gold OA. Decline it. Choose subscription. If any screen asks for payment details, you have taken a wrong turn.

*Burns* participates in Research4Life waivers, but **Saudi Arabia does not qualify** — do not plan around a waiver.

## 3. The mandatory disclosures form — the thing everyone misses

*Burns* has a **journal-specific hard requirement** announced at the top of its Guide:

> "All authors must complete this disclosures statement and upload this file along with the manuscript. **This is mandatory for the submission process.**"

**Download:** https://legacyfileshare.elsevier.com/gfa/journal-specific/burns-declarations.docx

It contains exactly six headings, all of which you must fill in (mark inapplicable ones "Not applicable"):

1. **Funding** → "This research did not receive any specific grant from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors."
2. **Clinical Trial Number** → Not applicable.
3. **Ethics Approval (IRB number as applicable)** → this study used only publicly available, de-identified images (Roboflow, CC BY 4.0) and an independent public research database (BIP_US, University of Seville); no new human-subjects data were collected; institutional review board approval was not required. **Attach or quote a letter from your faculty confirming this if you can get one — a burn journal will scrutinise this harder than an engineering journal.**
4. **Use of Generative AI** → see §4.
5. **Informed Consent** → Not applicable; no new participants, images are public and de-identified.
6. **CRediT Author Statement** → paste the authoritative version from `../../SUBMISSION/SUBMISSION_METADATA.md`.

**A submission without this file will be returned.**

## 4. The generative-AI declaration

Elsevier requires the narrative statement **inside the manuscript**, in a specific place, plus the field on the disclosures form. Both.

> "a statement at the end of the manuscript … **placed in a new section before the references list**"
> — Burns Guide for Authors, verified 2026-07-25

**Section title, verbatim as Elsevier prescribes it:**

> Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process

**Template, verbatim:**

> "During the preparation of this work the author(s) used [NAME OF TOOL / SERVICE] in order to [REASON]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the published article."

Your existing declaration already follows this template and ends with the required sentence. **Move it from the title page to a section immediately before the reference list. Do not shorten it.**

Two more Elsevier rules that apply to you:
- **AI-generated figures must be disclosed twice** — "in each image caption as well as in the general Generative AI disclosure statement." None of your figures are AI-generated images, but if any figure was *drawn* by a model, say so in its caption.
- **AI as research method is out of scope of this policy** — "This policy does not prevent the use of AI tools in formal research design or research methods." Your segmentation and classification models are the research, not a writing aid, and do not belong in this declaration. Keep the declaration about the writing and analysis assistance.

## 6. What to change in the manuscript for *Burns*

**Budget 4–6 weeks.** This is a clinical journal read by burn surgeons, nurses and researchers. The current manuscript is written for engineers. Almost every framing decision inverts.

**Lead with patient safety, not with methodology.**

| | Current paper | *Burns* version |
|---|---|---|
| **Headline finding** | A plain classifier beats the two-stage pipeline | **A burn-severity model that scores 82.6% internally under-grades 55 of 94 clinical burns, and calls half of full-thickness burns first degree** |
| Abstract | 321 words, benchmark-first | **≤250 words**, unstructured, clinical-first. Open with the safety finding |
| §1 Introduction | WHO statistics, then ML gaps | Keep the clinical opening (it is already good) and **expand the inter-rater agreement framing** — 60–80% surgeon agreement is the number this readership argues about |
| Deployed system | A subsection with Flutter/Flask | Keep, but frame as "a point-of-care smartphone tool of the kind increasingly proposed for low-resource settings" — and be blunt that it is not ready |
| Architecture benchmarking | Tables 2–4, extensive | **Compress heavily.** Burn surgeons do not care about ConvNeXt vs Swin. One table, one paragraph. Move the rest to supplementary |
| Leakage story | The intellectual centre | **Keep, but reframe as a warning to the burn-AI literature**: this is why published single-dataset accuracies in the 90s should not be believed. Cite Fu's five-year systematic review of burn CNN papers here — it is your bridge to this audience |
| BIP_US external result | §Res:ext | **Promote to the primary result**, with the diverging-bar figure of under- vs over-grading (BIP_US 55:2 against clean-external 51:45). This is the figure a clinician will remember |
| Discussion | Methodological lessons | Lead with **clinical implications**: under-triage directs a deep burn toward conservative care and delays referral to a burn centre. Say what the consequence is for a patient, explicitly |
| Efficiency table | Present | Cut or move to supplementary |
| Limitations | Good | **Add skin tone explicitly and prominently.** Pigmentation confounds erythema-based depth assessment; this readership knows that better than you do, and an unaddressed fairness limitation will be raised by every reviewer |

**Terminology to switch:**
- "first / second / third degree" → also give **"superficial, superficial-partial-thickness, deep-partial-thickness, full-thickness"**, which is what clinicians actually use. Your BIP_US mapping discussion becomes much more natural in this vocabulary.
- "under-graded" → **"under-triaged"** or "under-estimated burn depth" reads better clinically.
- "external validation set" → "independent clinical dataset".
- Drop: "leak-free", "oracle upper bound", "architecture-seed pairs", "MDE". Explain "data leakage" in one plain sentence the first time you use it.

**Consider voluntarily reporting against CLAIM or TRIPOD+AI.** *Burns* has **no** AI/ML-specific checklist — the Guide contains zero occurrences of CLAIM, TRIPOD, STARD, PROBAST or STROBE (verified by full-text search, 2026-07-25). Nothing obliges you and nothing forbids you. Voluntarily attaching a CLAIM checklist is a free credibility win with a clinical readership and there is no journal checklist to conflict with it. The guidelines *Burns* does name are CONSORT 2025, PRISMA 2020, ARRIVE, SAGER and ICMJE — none applies to you.
